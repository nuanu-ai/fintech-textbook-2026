# Timeline: what changed after the knowledge cutoff

The main text of this textbook has a knowledge cutoff of 15 September 2026. This timeline records, week by week, what changed worldwide in payments and fintech after that date: rules and regulators, compliance and anti-money laundering and customer checks (AML/KYC), licensing, payment schemes and networks, open banking, stablecoins and crypto regulation, central bank digital currencies (CBDC), AI-agent payments, and major launches and closures. It covers the EU and UK, the United States and Canada, Latin America, Asia-Pacific, the Middle East and Türkiye, Africa, Russia and the CIS, and international bodies.

When a change makes a statement in a lesson outdated, the lesson keeps its text and gets a dated note right below that statement, and the change is listed in [Outdated facts](Outdated-facts.md). Lesson text is never deleted. Smaller additions get a dated "Update" note in the lesson and appear under "Changes to lessons" in the week where they happened.

## How to read an entry

Each entry has the same shape:

```
- **YYYY-MM-DD · Jurisdiction · topic · stage.** What happened and what it means for payments, in one or two sentences. [Publisher — exact document title, YYYY-MM-DD](link) → [lesson N.N](link to the lesson)
```

- The date is the event date stated by the primary source. A scheduled change appears in the week it takes effect.
- The jurisdiction is the authority or organization that acted; a sanction by one country on firms in another is listed under the country that imposed it.
- The link in square brackets is the primary source. Titles in other languages are given in the original, with an English translation in parentheses.
- The arrow points to the closest lesson. An entry without an arrow has no close lesson.

Stages:

- **published** — a document is out; it does not by itself change anyone's obligations.
- **consultation** — a draft is open for comments and can still change.
- **proposed** — a formal draft rule or bill exists; it is not law yet.
- **adopted** — the authority has made the rule; it may apply only later.
- **in force / applies from** — the rule binds from this date, or its obligations start on this date.
- **launched** — the system or product is live for at least some users.
- **pilot / trial** — live but limited in users, value or time; not general availability.
- **closed** — a system, program, transitional period or consultation has ended.
- **enforcement** — a regulator acted against a named firm: a fine, an order or a ban.
- **sanctions** — a government listed persons or entities, freezing their assets or banning dealings with them.

## Rules

- **Primary sources only.** Every entry rests on a regulator, central bank, official journal, standard-setter, payment scheme, company newsroom or official specification repository. News sites are used only to find events.
- **A stated date.** An entry needs a primary source and a date that the source itself states. Reports without a reachable primary source, and dates we would have to calculate, are left out and counted in each week's "Coverage and limits".
- **Checked twice.** Every entry was checked against its primary source by a second agent.
- **Gaps are visible.** Each week lists the regions and sites that were checked and those that could not be reached. A region with no entry is a gap in coverage, not proof that nothing happened.
- **Teaching material.** The timeline is prepared by agents for teaching. It is not legal advice.
- **Updated every week.**
