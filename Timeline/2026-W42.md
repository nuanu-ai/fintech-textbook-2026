- **2026-10-16 · EU · crypto-asset service providers holding client funds under MiCA · published (Q&A).** The European Securities and Markets Authority (ESMA) published Q&A 2949 on crypto-asset service providers (CASPs) holding clients' funds in payment accounts at non-bank payment service providers (e-money and payment institutions). ## United States and Canada

- **2026-10-13 · United States · anti-money laundering and sanctions enforcement against a bank · enforcement.** Treasury announced more than $1.21 billion in combined penalties against Bank of America, N.A.: the Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN) assessed $1.2 billion for failure to mitigate and report priority national-security risks (Chinese money laundering networks, southwest-border illicit finance), the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency (OCC) assessed a $750 million civil money penalty for a deficient Bank Secrecy Act and anti-money laundering program, and the Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC) announced an $11.6 million settlement over apparent violations of numerous sanctions programs (including Iran, Russia, narcotics and global terrorism). The FinCEN consent order credits the OCC's $750 million payment against FinCEN's $1.2 billion, so the bank pays $450 million to the Treasury under that order; the total of more than $1.21 billion is FinCEN's $1.2 billion plus OFAC's settlement. In the FinCEN resolution the bank admits it willfully violated the Bank Secrecy Act; Treasury says the bank failed to report over $5 billion of suspicious transactions on time, and that the actions include the third-largest penalty in Treasury history and the largest in OCC history. The consent order requires a third-party lookback and independent reviews of transaction monitoring, customer due diligence and foreign-correspondent due diligence. The OCC order was not opened, so the OCC amount comes from the Treasury and FinCEN releases and the consent order; OFAC's own notice gives its settlement as $11,662,930 for 475 apparent violations; and neither the Treasury nor the FinCEN release names a payment channel such as Zelle. ## Latin America

- **2026-10-14 · Argentina · crypto service providers (PSAV) · adopted (published 2026-10-15; in force 2026-10-16).** The securities regulator, the Comisión Nacional de Valores (CNV), replaced the chapter of its rules on virtual asset service providers (PSAV) with General Resolution 1172/2026, signed on 2026-10-14, published in the Boletín Oficial on 2026-10-15 and in force the day after publication. It sets five activity categories (virtual asset–fiat exchange, virtual asset–virtual asset exchange, transfer, custody or administration, and financial services for issuers' offers), requires registration before operating, and sets minimum net equity of US$150,000 (categories 1, 2 and 4), US$75,000 (category 3) and US$35,000 (category 5), reducible by half depending on volume or custody over 12 months. Client assets and funds must be segregated and may not be used for the provider's own account, offering mixers or other anonymization services is prohibited, and records are kept for 10 years; a monthly report is due within 15 calendar days of month-end even with zero activity, and art. 4 makes the periodic information of art. 40 b) applicable from 2026-11-01 (which report starts first is an inference, because art. 4 does not say "monthly").
