# Week 40 · 28 September – 4 October 2026

Events dated 2026-09-28 … 2026-10-04 by their primary source. Researched 2026-10-22; every entry was checked against its primary source by a second agent. Teaching edition with limitations — not legal advice.

## Highlights

- The European Payments Council delayed the 2026-11-15 end date for unstructured addresses in all its payment schemes and set no new date.
- A US Treasury interim final rule states that the GENIUS Act takes effect on 2027-01-18.
- The US designated the Russia-linked A7 network, and the Financial Crimes Enforcement Network proposed barring US institutions from transfers involving its sub-agents.
- Card surcharges end and lower interchange caps apply in Australia from 2026-10-01 under the Reserve Bank of Australia's March decision.
- A new US dollar stablecoin, OUSD, launched from a company founded by Coinbase, Mastercard, Shopify, Stripe and Visa, and became Stripe's default stablecoin.

## United States and Canada

- **2026-09-30 · United States · state stablecoin regimes (GENIUS Act) · interim final rule in force.** The Stablecoin Certification Review Committee, chaired by the Treasury Secretary, adopted procedures and forms for states to certify that their payment stablecoin regimes are "substantially similar" to the federal one; certifications will be accepted only after a later Treasury notice. The rule states that the GENIUS Act "takes effect on January 18, 2027"; comments are due 2026-11-30. [Federal Register 2026-19966 — Forms and Procedures for Review of State Certifications by the Stablecoin Certification Review Committee, 2026-09-30](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-09-30/html/2026-19966.htm) → [lesson 1.4](../Textbook/01-Market-rules-and-licensing/README.md#module-1-4)
- **2026-10-01 · United States · sanctions on a Russia-linked payment network · sanctions; rule proposed.** The Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC) designated the A7 Network, which Treasury describes as a Russia-linked shadow banking network used by Iran, as a significant transnational criminal organization; the Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN) issued an alert and proposed barring covered US financial institutions from any transfer of funds involving A7 "sub-agents", including transfers to or from crypto addresses held for them. The proposal describes A7A5 as a ruble-backed stablecoin on Tron and Ethereum; it was published in the Federal Register on 2026-10-05, with comments due 2026-11-04. [FinCEN — Operation Economic Outcast Takes Unprecedented Action Against Sanctions Evasion Network Used by Iran, 2026-10-01](https://www.fincen.gov/news/news-releases/operation-economic-outcast-takes-unprecedented-action-against-sanctions-evasion); [U.S. Treasury — Operation Economic Outcast Takes Unprecedented Action Against Sanctions Evasion Network Used by Iran, 2026-10-01](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0644); [OFAC — Recent Actions: Iran-related Designations and Designations Updates; Transnational Criminal Organizations Designation, 2026-10-01](https://ofac.treasury.gov/recent-actions/20261001); [Federal Register 2026-20371 — Proposal of Special Measure Prohibiting the Transmittal of Funds Regarding Transactions Involving the A7 Network's Sub-Agents, 2026-10-05](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-10-05/html/2026-20371.htm) → [lesson 1.5](../Textbook/01-Market-rules-and-licensing/README.md#module-1-5), [lesson 6.5](../Textbook/06-Crypto-and-stablecoins/README.md#module-6-5)
- **2026-10-01 · United States · crypto custody by investment advisers and funds · proposed rule.** The Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) proposed custody rules that would set how regulated investment companies may hold crypto securities and similar investments, and how registered investment advisers may safeguard client crypto assets. Comments are due 2026-12-07. The proposal concerns advisers and funds, not payment stablecoin issuers. [SEC — Adviser and Regulated Fund Custody Rules; Crypto Custody Rules (File No. S7-2026-35), 2026-10-01](https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/2026/10/s7-2026-35); [Federal Register 2026-20466, 2026-10-06](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/06/2026-20466/adviser-and-regulated-fund-custody-rules-crypto-custody-rules) → [lesson 5.2](../Textbook/05-Wallets-accounts-and-cards/README.md#module-5-2)

- **Asia-Pacific:** checked central banks, supervisors and payment operators in Singapore, Hong Kong, mainland China, Japan, South Korea, Taiwan, India, Indonesia, the Philippines, Malaysia, Thailand and Australia; Vietnam, New Zealand and several securities regulators were not reached, and Australia's real-time payments operator was not checked.
- **Middle East and Türkiye:** checked UAE federal and free-zone regulators, the Saudi central bank and market regulator, the CBK, the Bank of Israel and Türkiye's Official Gazette and capital markets bulletins; Bahrain, Oman and Qatar's central bank sites could not be read. 1 report without a reachable primary source was left out.
- **Africa:** checked the BCEAO and the central banks or regulators of Nigeria, Kenya, South Africa, Egypt, the Central African monetary union, Ethiopia and Tanzania, plus PAPSS and Afreximbank; Ghana, Morocco, Rwanda and Uganda were not covered.
- **Russia and CIS:** checked Bank of Russia feeds and the central banks or regulators of Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan and Georgia; the State Duma, the Mir card operator and the central banks of Kyrgyzstan, Belarus, Armenia, Azerbaijan and Tajikistan could not be read. The US sanctions on the Russia-linked A7 network are listed under United States and Canada.
- **International bodies:** checked the FATF, the Financial Stability Board, the International Organization of Securities Commissions, the International Monetary Fund and the press and working-paper feeds of the Bank for International Settlements; most of that bank's pages were blocked, and the World Bank, the G7 and ISO were not checked. 3 reports without a reachable primary source were left out.
- **Global networks and AI-agent payments:** checked the Visa, Mastercard, Stripe, PayPal, Klarna and Swift newsrooms, the card industry's standards body EMVCo, web and internet standards bodies, and the repositories of the main agent payment protocols; Circle, Coinbase and Paxos could not be opened, and Adyen, Checkout.com, Revolut and Wise were not assessed. Unreleased draft changes in protocol repositories are not listed.
- **All regions:** a shared web-search budget ran out during research, so a region with no entry is a gap in coverage, not proof that nothing happened. In total, 8 reports without a reachable primary source were left out this week. Statistics, research papers, meetings, speeches, sandbox designations, consultation closing dates without an outcome and routine license decisions for small firms are not listed.
